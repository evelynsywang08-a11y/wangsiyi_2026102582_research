# Task 3: Importance of the Distinction Between “Hallmark Capability” and “Enabling Characteristic” 

Yes, the distinction is important, because it clarifies the causal hierarchy in Hanahan’s framework. A hallmark capability is a functional capacity that cancer cells or tumors actually acquire, such as sustained proliferation, immune evasion, or the newly proposed phenotypic plasticity and disrupted differentiation. An enabling characteristic, by contrast, is an upstream condition or mechanism that facilitates the acquisition of hallmark capabilities. In the 2022 paper, nonmutational epigenetic reprogramming and polymorphic microbiomes are examples of enabling characteristics. For instance, nonmutational epigenetic reprogramming can alter gene expression programs and make it easier for tumor cells to acquire multiple capabilities, such as drug resistance and immune evasion. Polymorphic microbiomes can act through inflammation, immune modulation, and metabolites, also enabling tumor progression. These are not themselves a single hallmark capability; they are enabling conditions. By contrast, phenotypic plasticity and disrupted differentiation are directly observable capabilities of tumor cells. Therefore, the distinction matters for causal reasoning, experimental design, and therapeutic targeting. However, Hanahan’s framework is heuristic and provisional, so the boundary is not absolute; some enabling mechanisms may also manifest as capabilities. Still, the distinction is conceptually useful.
